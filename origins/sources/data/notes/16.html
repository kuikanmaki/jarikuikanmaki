Later developments in the WHO Origins investigations: SAGO

Following the international backlash to the first WHO Wuhan report, China rejected the proposed Phase 2 investigation in July 2021. The WHO then established the Scientific Advisory Group for the Origins of Novel Pathogens (SAGO). In its final 2025 report, SAGO concluded that the origin of SARS-CoV-2 remains inconclusive due to missing key data, and reaffirmed that both zoonotic spillover and a laboratory leak must remain under consideration: Independent assessment of the origins of SARS-CoV-2, WHO SAGO, June 27, 2025.

Notably, SAGO never examined the DARPA DEFUSE proposal or related programs such as the Global Virome Project (GVP), China National Virome Project (CHVP), or USAID PREDICT and their connections to Wuhan. The two largest funders of the WHO, and therefore of SAGO itself, were the United States and China, the two nations most directly implicated in these programs. As of 2026, SAGO’s position remains unchanged: SAGO members (23 of 27), COVID’s origins: what we do and don’t know, Nature, February 24, 2026.

For the full release of the Year-4 RPPR, Year-5 Interim RPPR, and final RPPR for the grant “Understanding the Risk of Bat Coronavirus Emergence,” see Chapter 17 NIH FOIA library 2021. Despite NIH’s denials, the experiments described in the Year-4 Progress Report meet the standard scientific definition of gain-of-function research. The chimeric virus replicated to higher levels and caused more severe disease in human-ACE2 mice than the control. NIH later claimed the results were “unexpected,” but the experiment’s design made increased virulence entirely foreseeable. For more on this, see Chapter 14 Lerner & Hvistendahl 2021; Intercept FOIA 2021.
