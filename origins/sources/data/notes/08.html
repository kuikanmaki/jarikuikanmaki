Notes to Chapter 8


Note on Source 7

The European Commission’s guide explicitly classified claims that SARS-CoV-2 was artificially created or resulted from laboratory manipulation as dangerous conspiracy theories. Later reporting revealed, that Germany’s intelligence service BND had already assessed an 80–90% likelihood that the virus resulted from a laboratory accident involving gain-of-function research at the Wuhan Institute of Virology in spring 2020 (Chapter 19 Burger & Rinke 2025). As of 2026, the Commission’s original guide remains online without changes or retractions, while the BND’s intelligence report remains classified.


Beijing’s Influence on the EU

Contemporary reporting also suggested EU officials softened their language on Chinese disinformation under pressure from Beijing, a claim Brussels denied: Apuzzo, Matt, Pressured by China, E.U. Softens Report on Covid-19 Disinformation, New York Times, April 24, 2020. Notably, an archived version of the Wuhan Institute of Virology’s pre-pandemic website also lists the European Union as one of its official international partners: Wuhan Institute of Virology, Partnerships, Official English Website, May 29, 2019, archived copy.


Note on Source 16

In late April 2020, the US Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) stated that the intelligence community agreed with the scientific consensus that SARS-CoV-2 was “not manmade or genetically modified.” However, a later declassified Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) genome analysis from June 2020 already appears to contradict that public assessment (Chapter 19 DIA 2020 / USRTK FOIA release 2025).


Note on Source 19

The chapter references studies listed on the Project Evidence website. One of those studies, from 2019, describes experiments in which piglets were inoculated with a chimeric bat coronavirus. Another relevant study co-authored by Shi Zhengli is listed in Chapter 12 Sources (Zhou et al. 2018).

Early Pandemic Response

The chapter mentions several major issues from the first wave of the pandemic, including early government responses, public health assessments of symptoms and transmission of COVID-19, civil liberties, and retrospective evaluations of lockdown policies.

For additional reading about these topics, see for example: Hale, Thomas, et al., Variation in Government Responses to COVID-19, Oxford COVID-19 Government Response Tracker, 2020, Transmission of SARS-CoV-2: Implications for Infection Prevention Precautions, WHO, July 9, 2020, Democracy under Lockdown: The Impact of COVID-19 on Global Freedom, Freedom House, October 2020, Herby, Jonas, et al., Were COVID-19 lockdowns worth it? A meta-analysis, Public Choice, November 28, 2024.
