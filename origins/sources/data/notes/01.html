Notes to Chapter 1


Naming of SARS-CoV-2

The virus was initially designated 2019-nCoV (novel coronavirus 2019). It received its official name, SARS-CoV-2, on 11 February, 2020.


Note on Source 4: Key Figure: Edward Holmes

The Australian virologist who first published the SARS-CoV-2 genome outside China plays a significant role later in the origin investigation. He was one of the co-authors of the influential “Proximal Origin of SARS-CoV-2” paper (Chapter 6 Andersen et al. 2020). For later revelations concerning his collaborations with Wuhan Institute of Virology scientists and questions surrounding the publication of key virus genomes, see Chapter 19.


Note on Source 11: Key Figure: Ralph Baric

In this early article about the Wuhan outbreak, Ralph Baric, a leading coronavirus researcher at the University of North Carolina, was interviewed. The article also mentions his collaborative coronavirus research grant in Wuhan: “Wuhan is the epicenter for a lot of virology research in China. They have state of the art BSL3 and BSL4 facilities and world-class facilities to do anything that you would want to do on new emerging infectious diseases. So it’s occurring in the right spot,” Baric said.

Notably, in the follow-up article just four days later (Chapter 2 Branswell 2020), all references to the Wuhan Institute of Virology, its BSL-4 laboratory, and Baric’s collaborations in Wuhan had been removed. Instead, the article quoted Baric on the need to investigate the animals at the Huanan Seafood Market. For later revelations about Baric’s chimeric bat coronavirus research in Wuhan, see Chapters 12, 14, and 19.
