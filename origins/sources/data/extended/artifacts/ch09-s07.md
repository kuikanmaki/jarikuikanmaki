# Fauci accepts offer of chief medical adviser role in Biden administration

This article is more than 5 years old

- ‘I said yes right on the spot,’ says infectious diseases expert
- Fauci asked to continue as member of coronavirus taskforce

Anthony Fauci, the US’s top infectious disease expert in the country’s response to the coronavirus, told NBC News on Friday that he “absolutely” accepted Joe Biden’s offer to join his administration and serve as chief medical adviser.

“I said yes right on the spot,” Fauci [told the Today Show’s Savannah Guthrie](https://twitter.com/TODAYshow/status/1334837962060730369).

On Thursday, in an interview with CNN’s Jake Tapper, the president-elect confirmed that he had asked Fauci to continue his role on Donald Trump’s coronavirus taskforce when he takes office 20 January.

“I asked him to stay on in the exact same role as he’s had for the past several presidents, and I asked him to be a chief medical adviser for me as well and be part of the Covid-19 team,” said Biden.

Fauci, the director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, has had a difficult relationship with President Trump, who had [threatened to fire him after November’s elections](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/nov/02/donald-trump-threatens-to-fire-anthony-fauci-after-us-election).

After public statements disagreeing with or contradicting the president on wearing masks, social distancing and public gatherings, he became the target of Trump’s attacks.

Throughout his campaign, however, Biden showed support for Fauci, imploring Americans to follow the guidance of a trusted expert over the president’s often-debunked conspiracy theories.

Biden said he spoke with Fauci about staying on by stressing the need to build American confidence and trust in any coronavirus vaccine. He also argued the country doesn’t “have to close down the economy” in order to stop the spread.

The news comes as coronavirus cases across the country surge during the holiday season. Fauci and other public health experts have cautioned Americans to avoid travel and large gatherings altogether.

## This is just the beginning …

For two years, Americans have witnessed a largely unchecked assault on the foundations of US democracy. Authoritarian leadership has enriched itself through corruption, spread lies and launched relentless attacks on press freedom, voting rights, due process and many fundamental civil rights.

Last night, America’s embattled embattled democracy came roaring back to life: from Texas to Ohio to South Carolina, Americans surged to the polls to fight for a different path forward. The process worked, the people spoke, the candidates – and the president – accepted the results. Today, we’re on the cusp of a new chapter for America, where the people have claimed their right to hold power to account, and millions are looking at politics with a new and unfamiliar feeling of hope.

The past two years have delivered a chilling lesson in just how fragile our fundamental freedoms are, when precious few powerful institutions are willing to stand up for the truth and defend our basic rights and freedoms. Major law firms and universities capitulated to a bullying president’s threats and intimidation. Some corporate news outlets bowed to his pressure, too.

In the face of these attacks, the independent press stood out as one of the only forces for accountability, dedicated to uncovering the truth – and making sure the public understood what was at stake in this election.

At its best, a truly free press can speak directly to the public, shedding light on hidden schemes and revealing the whole picture of our flawed world. Yet these are challenging times for press freedom: Trump’s crackdown on the press shows no signs of letting up; consolidated ownership means fewer options and less diversity of perspective; and the advent of AI has threatened the entire business model of journalism, resulting in extreme staff layoffs that have gutted newsrooms.

We will need a free press in 2027 as much as we relied on one in 2025 and 2026. The Guardian is fortunate to have no billionaire owner, no corporate bosses who put profit over mission.
