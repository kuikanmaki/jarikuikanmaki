The Chinese laboratory at the centre of the coronavirus leak theory listed members of the Chinese military on its oversight committees and included international scientists without their knowledge, according to an investigation.

Fabrizio Gatti, an Italian author, said the Wuhan Institute of Virology deleted all of the names from its website last year after he contacted the scientists to ask them about their roles.

Professor Christian Bréchot, then vice-president of the Mérieux Institute in France, answered: “In fact, although they may have kept my name on the list of their advisory committee I have not been in contact with the Wuhan Institute of Virology since the years around 2012 … No I have not been contacted but I had no reason to be contacted.”

Xiao-Fan Wang, of the Duke University School of Medicine in North Carolina, said that he had advised the Chinese Academy of Sciences on safety standards but had not returned to Wuhan since 2013. “My research area is cancer and I have never worked in the field of virology,” he wrote.

Gatti said he had experience in investigating air accidents and the first step was to download as many relevant documents as possible. “The documents published in my book are strong evidence of what the US government is looking for. They have now all been deleted,” he said.

“These documents are an important starting point for an international investigation. The Chinese army is involved in this huge catastrophe so it is unlikely the Chinese will make them available.”

The claims come as US presses China to further investigate the possibility that the virus might have [escaped from the lab](https://www.thetimes.com/world/asia/article/g7-summit-2021-we-cant-rule-out-covid-lab-leak-says-who-chief-58j26k709), which is located in the same Chinese city that reported the first cases of Covid-19 in December 2019. Members of the Trump administration have also claimed that the lab was being used for military experiments. The Chinese government has denied the allegations.

It has cited findings by a World Health Organisation team that a leak was “extremely unlikely”, but the [Biden administration](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/boris-johnson-and-joe-biden-divided-over-coronavirus-leak-xksvb3fwk) has renewed the call for a further investigation and the president ordered US intelligence agencies to look into the origins of the virus.

Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, the chief of the World Health Organisation, has also urged a deeper inquiry into the lab leak hypothesis.

The lab has meanwhile been nominated for the Chinese Academy of Sciences’ 2021 Outstanding Science and Technology Achievement Prize, the country’s top science award, for its work on the pandemic.

Shi Zhengli, the researcher known as China’s “bat woman” for her extensive work on the animals, received special mention in the nomination.

In a rare interview with _The New York Times_, Shi expressed her frustration with the lab leak theories. “How on earth can I offer up evidence for something where there is no evidence?” she said. “I don’t know how the world has come to this, constantly pouring filth on an innocent scientist.”
