At the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center campus in Seattle, surrounded by sycophants, Dr. Anthony Fauci did what he does best: he talked about himself.

“It’s called the Fauci Effect,” he told the captivated audience that had gathered to watch Fauci receive yet another award.

In an attempt at self-deprecation, he added, “Trust me, I don’t get excited about that.”

After which he went on to get very excited indeed about the phenomenon that is allegedly sweeping the nation.

“People go to medical school now, people are interested in science — not because of me, because most people don’t know me, who I am … It’s what I symbolize.”

If your eyes are already in the back of your head, buckle up. It gets way worse.

“And what I symbolize in an era of the normalization of untruths and lies, and all the things you’re seeing going on in society, from Jan. 6 to everything else that goes on — people are craving for consistency, for integrity, for truth and for people caring about people.”

Fauci’s narcissism is consistent, I’ll give him that. It doesn’t matter if it is March 2020 or August 2022, no one has a higher opinion of Tony than Tony.

While you might disagree with the good doctor’s definition of the so-called “Fauci effect,” there is no denying that he has had quite the impact on our country.

Recently on Fox News, Neil Cavuto asked Fauci if he has any regrets about the lockdown and how it has irreparably damaged kids.

The thin-skinned superstar shot back at Cavuto that he doesn’t think the lockdowns “forever irreparably damaged anyone.”

Well, I’m not America’s favorite doctor — I don’t even play one on TV — but I’d like to offer a second opinion on the matter.

Let’s start with a study from the federal Department of Education this week.

The report showed that school shutdowns erased decades of advances in learning — especially among minority students. In fact, the “average scores for age 9 students in 2022 declined 5 points in reading” — the largest achievement drop since 1990.

No one was more instrumental in promoting lockdowns than Dr. Fauci, who is on record numerous times — thanks to his endless cable TV hits — advocating for virtual learning. He was constantly fear-mongering about kids getting infected if schools were to reopen too soon.

Now that the consequences of these cruel and unnecessary lockdowns are becoming more and more irrefutable, the director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases is trying to rewrite history.

Learning loss is just the tip of the iceberg when it comes to the results of two years of what Bari Weiss, a former Wall Street Journal and New York Times opinion writer and editor, accurately dubbed a “catastrophic moral crime” in an appearance on Bill Maher.

The CDC reported that during 2020, “the proportion of mental health–related emergency department (ED) visits among adolescents aged 12–17 years increased 31% compared with that during 2019.”

The same report noted that “during February 21–March 20, 2021, suspected suicide attempt (emergency department) visits were 50.6% higher among girls aged 12–17 years than during the same period in 2019.”

But Fauci doesn’t think people were irreparably damaged? I guess he reserves his hysteria for topics like double-masking and Super Bowl super-spreaders, not for young women trying to kill themselves.

As Fauci was posing for cringe-worthy magazine covers and throwing out that laughable first pitch on Opening Day, plenty of Americans were dealing with the fallout of the shutdowns.

While Fauci reveled in his stardom and basked in the glory of the bright lights at CNN, less fortunate Americans couldn’t visit their sick loved ones in hospitals or nursing homes.

While Fauci received the Presidential Medal of Freedom and watched as his face was stitched on pillows and socks, many business owners watched their doors close for good — unable to stay afloat after months of shuttered storefronts.

While Fauci giggled over Brad Pitt’s fawning impersonation of him on Saturday Night Live, families mourned missed holidays, weddings, funerals, graduations, dinners, sports games and school plays.

Dr. Fauci, America’s highest paid federal employee after 55 years at the public trough, is now on a farewell tour. Shameless “journalists” thank him for his public service and slobber all over their favorite megalomaniac. Their hagiographic coverage of Fauci remains, well, consistent.

But most people have heard enough from the 81-year-old power-hungry tyrant.

A poll taken in April from the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation and the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health showed that only 37% of Americans had “a lot of trust in the National Institutes of Health.”

Other public health groups fared almost as poorly. Expect that distrust of public health “experts” to continue for a very long time… consistently.

I’d chalk it up to the Fauci effect.
